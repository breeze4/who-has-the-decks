    Telling them apart

    The film cuts between several cameras, so left and right swap from shot to shot. Use what they are wearing, not where they stand.

    Which is which is taken from the clothing: the shirt with “again” on the sleeve is the tour’s own. That is a contextual read from four public stills of the video, not a confirmed identification. If you know them by sight and it is the other way round, every colour on this page flips.

    How to read who is doing what

    Sound is continuous in the film but the picture is not, so you will never see every move. These are the tells that work when the camera is on the booth, strongest first.

    1. The drum machine is Thomas

      Fred says a drum machine was synced to the decks and Thomas played it all night. Any percussion or synth sound that is not part of the record is him. If he is bent over a box that is neither a deck nor the mixer, that is what he is doing.

      From Fred’s notes

    2. One headphone cup on an ear

      The person listening in headphones is lining up the next track, which makes the next transition theirs. Headphones round the neck means that person is not cueing.

      General DJ practice

    3. A hand on a deck

      Nudging a platter, moving the tempo slider, pressing cue or play, or scrolling the deck’s screen to pick a track. Whoever does that owns whatever comes out of that deck next.

      General DJ practice

    4. Two hands on the mixer during a change

      In a blend, one hand takes the bass out of the outgoing channel while the other brings the incoming channel up. The person doing that is performing the transition, even if the other one chose the record.

      General DJ practice

    5. Hands off, looking up

      Arms in the air, laughing, or looking at the crowd means it is the other person’s move. Fred’s notes describe him looking out at the crowd at 36:04 and being surprised by a file name at 41:15.

      General DJ practice

    The catch: many of the “blends” are single files

    Fred writes that the two of them spent the week in a London studio and arrived with “a usb stacked with ideas”. About twenty Technologic mash-ups were made in advance. One deck showed a file called “revolution yeah”. The Billie and Digital Love mash-up was filmed in the studio that afternoon.

    So when three records are playing at once, that does not mean someone is juggling three decks. Often one of them pressed play on a prepared file. The live decisions are which idea to pull, when, how to get in and out of it, and what Thomas adds on the drum machine. Fred also says some ideas were made “without each other hearing so it was still a surprise”, which is why his reactions are a useful tell.

    Limits

    • The video could not be played or downloaded where this guide was built, so nothing here was checked against the picture or the sound. Hand positions, effects, EQ moves and which deck each track is on are all still open.
    • Timestamps are Fred’s. One entry (Les Amazones, about 1:16:48) has no time in his notes and uses an automatic detector’s estimate; it is marked “approx”.
    • Tempo and key figures are not given per track, because the only ones available are machine estimates.
    • Descriptions of how a blend works musically are reasoning from the tracklist, and say so.
